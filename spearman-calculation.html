Worked follow-up

Calculate a Spearman score by hand

Use four jobs to see how a single change in order affects the score.

Rank the two columns

Order payload sizes from smallest to largest, starting at 1. Do the same for latency. Subtract the latency rank from the payload rank in each row, then square the difference.

Turn the differences into a score

For ranks with no ties, multiply the sum of squared differences by 6 and divide by the number of rows multiplied by (the number of rows squared minus 1). Subtract the result from 1.

1 − 6 × 2 ÷ [4 × (4² − 1)] = 0.80

The positive score shows that larger payloads generally go with longer jobs. It uses order alone, so changing 140 to 400 ms keeps the same ranks and score. If two values tie for second and third place, give both rank 2.5. Calculate Pearson correlation on the two rank columns when there are ties.

Try it

On paper or in a spreadsheet, copy the payload values. Beside them, write 140, 105, 90 and 75 ms in that order. Predict the score, calculate it, then open the answer.

Answer for the reversed ranks

The latency ranks are 4, 3, 2, 1. The squared differences are 9, 1, 1, 9. The score is 1 − 6 × 20 ÷ 60 = −1, because the orders reverse exactly.
