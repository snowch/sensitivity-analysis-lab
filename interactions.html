Lesson 3 of 4

Find the pipeline interaction

Compare what the rank screen and Sobol indices reveal when the queue adds a delay only at high arrivals and low capacity.

Arrival rate is how many jobs arrive per second. Worker capacity is how many jobs the pipeline can process per second. The model can add 155 ms of queue delay at 750 arrivals per second or more and 800 jobs per second of capacity or less. When the effect of one input depends on the value of another, they interact.

Variance measures how far outcomes spread around their average and gives extra weight to large deviations. A first-order Sobol index estimates the share of variance explained by an input on its own. Its total-order index also includes every interaction involving that input. A score of 0.70 represents roughly 70% of the modelled variance under the chosen input ranges.

Does the queue change which inputs matter?

  1. Leave Scenario set to Baseline and select Run sensitivity analysis. In Sobol indices and latency, compare the First-order and Total-order values for Cache hit rate. How large is the gap with the penalty off?
  2. Select Queue saturation, then run again at the same sample size. Compare First-order and Total-order for Arrival rate and Worker capacity. Does either have a bigger gap than cache hit rate did? Use Spearman rank to check whether the rank screen places both near the top.
  3. Compare p95 latency in Latency across scenarios, which appears after both runs. p95 is the time by which at least 95% of the sampled jobs finish. Use Jobs with extra delay to see how often the penalty occurs. How does that fraction help explain the change in p95?

Read the result

When the total-order bar is much longer than the first-order bar, the input’s interactions account for much of its contribution. The Sobol bars describe variance across all jobs. Read p95 for a separate view of the slowest jobs.

Reveal the leading inputs and p95 for both scenarios

With the penalty off, cache hit rate leads. With the penalty on, arrival rate and worker capacity lead by total order. The penalty affects about six in 100 jobs, enough to move p95 from about 124 to 239 ms.

Sampling method and limits of the queue model

The experiment draws independent inputs into two samples. For each input it swaps values between the samples and reruns the model to estimate the two indices. Small negative first-order estimates can occur because the sample is finite. Total-order shares can add to more than one because two inputs can both contribute to the same interaction.

Each condition for the queue penalty covers a quarter of its input range. Under independent draws they coincide about one time in sixteen. The step penalty illustrates a slowdown and would need separate validation against a real queue.
