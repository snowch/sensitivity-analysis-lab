Calculate Sobol indices for two switches

Use four equally likely outcomes to separate individual effects from an interaction.

List all four outcomes

Switches A and B each turn on with probability one half. Each switch’s chance of being on stays the same when you know the other switch’s state. The output is 1 when both are on and 0 in the other cases.

Compare the two sources of spread

See how the four outputs split into two groups before calculating the shares.

  1. All outputs. The mean is 0.25. Three outputs lie 0.25 below the mean and one lies 0.75 above it. Average their squared distances to get (3 × 0.25² + 0.75²) ÷ 4 = 0.1875.
  2. A alone. The two group averages for A, 0 and 0.5, are equally likely. Each sits 0.25 from the mean. The variance of those averages is 0.25² = 0.0625.
  3. First-order index. Divide 0.0625 by 0.1875 to get 1/3. B has the same index.

The two first-order effects account for 2/3 of the variance. The remaining 1/3 belongs to their interaction. A’s total-order index includes its own 1/3 and the interaction’s 1/3, giving 2/3. Each switch’s total includes that same interaction.

Recalculate when the output counts switches

On paper, set the output to the number of switches that are on. Write the four outputs, group them by A’s state and calculate A’s first- and total-order indices. For switch A, subtract its first-order index from its total-order index. Is that gap smaller or larger than under the original rule, which outputs 1 only when both switches are on? Open Answer for the switch-count rule to check.

Answer for the switch-count rule

The outputs are 0, 1, 1, 2. Their variance is 0.5. A off and A on give averages of 0.5 and 1.5, with variance 0.25. A’s first-order index is 0.25 ÷ 0.5 = 1/2. B explains the other half, so A’s total order is also 1/2. Each switch always adds one, whatever the other switch does.
