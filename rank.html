Rank the knobs

How can you make a first shortlist when 13 inputs could matter?

Draw possible runs and compare their ranks

Start with a formula for end-to-end latency and a plausible range for each input. A Monte Carlo simulation repeatedly draws values from those ranges, runs the formula, and records the result. Here every value within a range is equally likely, and each input is sampled independently.

Spearman rank correlation orders the runs twice, once by the input and once by latency. Similar order gives a score near +1, reverse order gives a score near −1, and little consistent ordering gives a score near zero. Look at the magnitude to shortlist inputs and the sign to read the direction.

Check your reasoning

A feature has Spearman ρ = −0.84. What can you say?

Calculate Spearman’s ρ by hand

Imagine four jobs, sorted by payload size. Their payload ranks are 1, 2, 3, 4. Their latency ranks, in that same row order, are 1, 3, 2, 4. The rank differences are 0, −1, 1, 0, so the squared differences sum to 2. With no ties, the shortcut is 1 − 6 × 2 ÷ (4 × (4² − 1)) = 0.8.

The general calculation is Pearson correlation on the ranks. Tied values get the average of the positions they share. Ranking drops information about distances. A 2 ms miss and a 200 ms miss can occupy adjacent positions.

When a small score can hide a big effect

Imagine fuel use is lowest at a moderate speed and rises both below and above it. With speeds spread symmetrically around that optimum, opposite slopes can cancel in Spearman’s ρ. Plot the input against the output before dropping a weakly ranked factor. Look for bends, thresholds and separate groups.

Spearman is useful for a first screen across many inputs. Use distribution or variance estimates when the decision concerns the spread or tail, and matched runs when you need the effect of a specified change.

Inspect the model and input ranges

The baseline adds a 12 ms fixed cost to the fetch, process, wait and write stages. Read the simulation source and rank and Sobol calculations.

Read the baseline result

With the supplied ranges, cache hit rate and payload size dominate. Higher hit rates reduce time spent on cache misses, while bigger payloads cost more to process. Spearman’s ρ describes monotonic association. Adding the scores cannot tell you how much variance the two inputs explain.

Put the smaller drivers aside for this baseline investigation. Keep the assumptions with your shortlist so you can revisit it when conditions change.
