Sensitivity depends on the range

Each extra millisecond of network round-trip time adds one millisecond to output latency. Across an 8–12 ms range, the network contribution can change by 4 ms. Across a 2–65 ms range, it can change by 63 ms. The same formula now faces a much wider set of possible conditions.

Pick ranges from the population your decision concerns. A local deployment and a global rollout may each need their own scenario. Reuse the random draws and change just one assumption so you can explain why the answer moved.

How ranges, distributions and dependencies change the result

A uniform draw gives equal probability to equal-width intervals. Its variance is the squared range width divided by 12. For this additive network term, widening the range from 4 to 63 ms multiplies its own variance by about 248. Its share of total variance also depends on every other input.

Ranges alone leave the shape open. A triangular distribution might fit an expert estimate with a clear most likely value, and empirical resampling can retain common operating clusters. If arrival rate and capacity rise together, independent draws can invent high-load, low-capacity combinations. Consider modelling the paired observations or separating operating regimes before applying independent-input Sobol indices.

Record where each range or distribution came from. For weak assumptions, compare plausible scenarios and check whether the decision survives. A ranking reversal identifies a measurement worth collecting.
