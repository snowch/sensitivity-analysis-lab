Lesson 3 of 4

Find the pipeline interaction

Compare what the rank screen and Sobol indices reveal when the queue adds a delay only at high arrivals and low capacity.

Find when the queue penalty applies

Arrival rate is how many jobs arrive per second. Worker capacity is how many jobs the pipeline can process per second.

The model adds 155 ms when at least 750 jobs arrive per second and worker capacity is at most 800 jobs per second. Both conditions must hold. Arrivals and capacity interact because the effect of one depends on the other.

Compare individual and total effects

Variance measures spread around the average and gives extra weight to large deviations. A first-order Sobol index estimates the share of variance explained by an input on its own.

The total-order index includes the input’s own share and every interaction involving it. For these independent inputs, a total-order score of 0.70 means that about 70% of modelled variance involves that input.

Does switching on the queue penalty change which inputs lead by total-order Sobol index?

Read the result

A large gap between total and first order means interactions account for much of the input’s contribution to variance. The Sobol bars describe variance across all jobs. The p95 shows where the slowest 5% of jobs begin.

Reveal the leading inputs and p95 for both scenarios

With the penalty off, cache hit rate leads. With the penalty on, arrival rate and worker capacity lead by total order. The penalty affects about six in 100 jobs, enough to move p95 from about 124 to 239 ms.

Check your reasoning

With the queue penalty on, arrival rate has a first-order index near 0.17 and a total-order index near 0.71. What would help explain the gap?

Sampling method and limits of the queue model

The experiment draws independent inputs into two samples. For each input it swaps values between the samples and reruns the model to estimate the two indices. Small negative first-order estimates can occur because the sample is finite. Total-order shares can add to more than one because two inputs can both contribute to the same interaction.

Each condition for the queue penalty covers a quarter of its input range. Under independent draws they coincide about one time in sixteen. Before using this model to choose a worker capacity, compare measured latencies for similar workloads just above and below the proposed arrival and capacity cutoffs. If latency grows gradually, test a gradual penalty and see whether it changes which capacity you would choose.
